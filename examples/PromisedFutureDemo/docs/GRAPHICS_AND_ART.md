# Graphics and art

The scene uses the core low-poly polygon renderer at 120×160 RGB565. A perspective camera looks across a continuous blue lagoon toward layered green islands and original tapering white/cyan arcologies. Faceted palms, cloud clusters, beacon structures, and broad water glints are real world-space geometry depth-tested against the scene. A saturated blue-to-aqua sky and light distance haze frame the skyline; there is no raycast hallway or 2D skyline overlay.

Each arcology is one continuous shell: the five rings that make up its silhouette are shared by the level below and the level above, and the leaning profile comes from a single per-ring offset table, so no joint carries a radius or centre step. The cyan glass stripes are decals sitting on the shell surface, separated from it by the core `depth_bias` polygon-offset field rather than by pushing a ring outside the silhouette.

A single sun, up and to the left in front of the camera, drives every illumination value in the scene. Ring corners take their light from the outward surface direction and the shell quads interpolate it per vertex (`vxpe_toon3d_quad_gouraud`), so the lit flank of each arcology, island and palm points the same way and the falloff across a face is continuous; the three cel bands still quantise the result. This replaced a per-quad value that varied with the ring index, which read as noise rather than light.

The sky is a dithered gradient (`vxpe2d_gradient_vertical_dithered`), because a 160-row cobalt-to-aqua band steps through few RGB565 levels and otherwise repeats the same row. In this scene the plain fill left 51 of the 80 sky rows identical to the row above at the sampled column; dithering brings that to 24, and on the core regression's wider metric (whole-row copies of a synthetic 64x64 band) it goes from 32 to 0. A warm radial glow sits in the sun's corner and a short haze band lifts the waterline, both painted before the depth buffer is cleared so world geometry occludes them normally. The lagoon glints swell on a triangle wave of the frame counter (no wrap pop, no allocation) with a different phase per sheet, and each island carries a surf line: a flat annulus one unit above the water that is separated from the water plane by the material depth bias instead of a taller offset.

The 120×160 RGB565 color target and matching per-pixel depth buffer use 76,800 bytes together. Scale2x fills the 240×320 device screen. Geometry is generated from compact fixed recipes; the hot frame path does no heap allocation, image decoding, or storage I/O. One full frame (world render, Scale2x and HUD) measures 1.877 ms on a host build; no device timing was taken, so treat that as a relative reference only. The core triangle rasterizer applies a top-left fill rule, so shared edges and coincident surfaces resolve from geometry instead of from submission order; `docs/TOWER_ZFIGHT_FINDINGS.md` records the measurements behind that change. Camera turn uses a 32-entry Q14 lookup table. Public cache stores only the four-bit beacon progress.

Controls: `2/8` walk, `4/6` turn, `5` scan/start, `0` journal, `9` pause. Walk near a beacon until its scan prompt appears. The versioned save uses the unique app folder `@vxp_003000791`, preferring removable storage and falling back to system storage.
